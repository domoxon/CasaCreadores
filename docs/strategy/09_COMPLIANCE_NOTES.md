## Crypto Content Compliance by Country

### The Core Problem
Crypto marketing is regulated differently in every LATAM country. Creators posting about crypto products on your network could inadvertently violate financial promotion laws — and if Casa Creadores brokered that deal, you may share liability.

**The solution:** Put clear responsibility on the brand in your contract, and give creators a mandatory compliance checklist per campaign.

### Brazil
- Regulator: CVM (Comissão de Valores Mobiliários)
- CVM has issued guidance that crypto assets offering returns may be classified as securities
- Creator posts must not promise returns, use language like "guaranteed profit," or imply investment advice
- Influencer marketing must be disclosed (#publi or #publicidade)
- Reference: https://www.gov.br/cvm

### Mexico
- Regulator: CNBV (Comisión Nacional Bancaria y de Valores) + Banxico
- Fintech Law (2018) regulates crypto operations
- Creator posts promoting crypto exchanges must not imply government backing or deposit insurance
- Mandatory: #publicidad disclosure
- Reference: https://www.cnbv.gob.mx

### Argentina
- Regulator: CNV (Comisión Nacional de Valores)
- CNV has been active in issuing warnings about crypto marketing
- Stablecoin and DeFi products exist in a grey area
- Creator posts must include clear risk disclosures
- Reference: https://www.cnv.gob.ar

### Colombia
- Regulator: SFC (Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia)
- Crypto not formally regulated as financial instruments but SFC has issued consumer warnings
- Influencer posts promoting crypto require #publicidad and risk disclaimer

### General Rule for All Countries
Require all crypto/financial campaign posts to include:
- Clear sponsorship disclosure (#ad, #publicidad, #publicidade, #patrocinado)
- Risk disclaimer: "Las criptomonedas son activos volátiles. Esto no es asesoramiento financiero."
- No price predictions or return guarantees
- No claims of regulatory approval unless verifiably true

## Influencer Marketing Disclosure Requirements

LATAM broadly follows international best practice (similar to FTC guidelines in the US):

**Required on every sponsored post:**
- Written disclosure visible without expanding the caption
- Must use clear language: #ad, #publicidad, #publicidade, #sponsored, #patrocinado
- Cannot be buried in a list of hashtags
- Stories: must have visible text overlay, not just verbal mention

**Platforms' own policies:**
- Instagram: Paid Partnership label (brand must be set up for this)
- TikTok: Branded Content toggle (required by TikTok ToS)
- YouTube: Must check "includes paid promotion" in video settings
- LinkedIn: No built-in tool — text disclosure in post copy required

**Include in every creator brief:** exact disclosure language to use and where.

## Casa Creadores Terms of Service — Key Clauses to Cover

When drafting your website Terms of Service and creator/brand contracts, ensure these are addressed:

### Platform Terms of Service
- Casa Creadores is not responsible for API data accuracy
- Calculator results are estimates only, not financial advice
- We use publicly available data only; no private account data is accessed

### Creator Agreement
- Creator is responsible for ensuring their content complies with local laws
- Creator must disclose sponsorships as required by platform and local regulation
- Creator grants brand a 12-month license to repurpose content for paid advertising
- Casa Creadores acts as agent, not employer — creator is independent contractor
- Payment disputes: raise within 7 days of campaign completion
- Casa Creadores reserves the right to remove creators from the network for policy violations

### Brand Agreement
- Brand is responsible for ensuring campaign claims are compliant with financial regulations in their target market
- Brand must not instruct creators to make false, misleading, or non-compliant claims
- Brand content guidelines must be submitted before campaign launch
- Casa Creadores is not liable for regulatory action arising from brand-directed content
- Cancellation and refund terms (50% deposit non-refundable if cancelled within 7 days of campaign start)

## Scraping & Data Usage

The calculator tool uses third-party scraping APIs to retrieve publicly available social media data. This is standard industry practice but technically violates the ToS of most platforms.

**Mitigation:**
- Only access public profile data
- Do not store scraped data beyond session/cache
- Add to website footer: "We only access publicly available data. We are not affiliated with Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, X, Twitch, or LinkedIn."
- Do not sell or commercialise the data retrieved

## Privacy & GDPR/LGPD

Brazil has the LGPD (Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados) — similar to GDPR. Argentina has the PDPA. Both are relevant.

**Practical steps at launch:**
- Privacy Policy on website (required) — template generators available free at iubenda.com or Termly.io
- Cookie consent banner (required for EU visitors, best practice for LATAM)
- Creator application data: stored in Tally (their servers), accessible to Casa Creadores team only
- No selling or sharing of creator/brand contact data with third parties
- Data deletion requests: respond within 30 days

## Practical Compliance Checklist for Every Campaign

Before a campaign goes live, confirm:

- [ ] Creator brief includes mandatory disclosure language
- [ ] Brand has confirmed no claims violate local financial regulation
- [ ] No price predictions or "guaranteed returns" language anywhere
- [ ] TikTok Branded Content toggle is on (for TikTok campaigns)
- [ ] Instagram Paid Partnership label requested (if brand has Instagram Business account)
- [ ] YouTube paid promotion checkbox reminder sent to creators
- [ ] Risk disclaimer text confirmed and included in brief
